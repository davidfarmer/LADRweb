Theorem 1.3. properties of complex arithmetic.

  1. commutativity.
    \(\alpha + \beta = \beta + \alpha\) and \(\alpha \beta = \beta \alpha\) for all \(\alpha , \beta \in \C{}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. associativity.
    \((\alpha + \beta) + \lambda = \alpha + (\beta + \lambda)\) and \((\alpha \beta)\lambda = \alpha (\beta \lambda)\) for all \(\alpha , \beta, \lambda \in \C{}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. identities.
    \(\lambda + 0 = \lambda\) and \(\lambda 1 = \lambda\) for all \(\lambda \in \C{}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. additive inverse.
    For every \(\alpha \in \C{}\text{,}\) there exists a unique \(\beta \in \C{}\) such that \(\alpha + \beta = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. multiplicative inverse.
    For every \(\alpha \in \C{}\) with \(\alpha \ne 0\text{,}\) there exists a unique \(\beta \in \C{}\) such that \(\alpha \beta = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  6. distributive property.
    \(\lambda(\alpha + \beta) = \lambda \alpha + \lambda \beta\) for all \(\lambda, \alpha , \beta \in \C{}\text{.}\)
