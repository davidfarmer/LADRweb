Example 8.15. nilpotent operators.

  1. The operator \(T \in \Lc( \F{4} )\) defined by
    \begin{equation*} T(z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4) = (0, 0, z_1, z_2) \end{equation*}
    is nilpotent because \(T^2 = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The operator on \(\F3\) whose matrix (with respect to the standard basis) is
    \begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix}-3 \amp 9 \amp 0 \\ -7 \amp 9 \amp 6 \\ 4 \amp 0 \amp -6 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    is nilpotent, as can be shown by cubing the matrix above to get the zero matrix.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The operator of differentiation on \(\P{}_m(\R{})\) is nilpotent because the \((m + 1)^{\nth}\) derivative of every polynomial of degree at most \(m\) equals \(0\text{.}\) Note that on this space of dimension \(m + 1\text{,}\) we need to raise the nilpotent operator to the power \(m + 1\) to get the \(0\) operator.
