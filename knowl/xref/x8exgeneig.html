Example 8.10. generalized eigenvectors of an operator on \(\C3\).

Define \(T \in \Lc( \C{3} )\) by
\begin{equation*} T(z_1, z_2, z_3) = (4z_2, 0, 5z_3) \end{equation*}
for each \((z_1, z_2, z_3) \in \CC3\text{.}\) A routine use of the definition of eigenvalue shows that the eigenvalues of \(T\) are \(0\) and \(5\text{.}\) Furthermore, the eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue \(0\) are the nonzero vectors of the form \((z_1, 0, 0)\text{,}\) and the eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue \(5\) are the nonzero vectors of the form \((0, 0, z_3)\text{.}\) Hence this operator does not have enough eigenvectors to span its domain \(\CC3\text{.}\)
🔗
We compute that \(T^3(z_1, z_2, z_3) = (0, 0, 125 z_3)\text{.}\) Thus 8.1 and Theorem 8.3 imply that the generalized eigenvectors of \(T\) corresponding to the eigenvalue \(0\) are the nonzero vectors of the form \((z_1, z_2, 0)\text{.}\)
🔗
We also have \((T-5I)^3(z_1, z_2, z_3) = (-125z_1 + 300z_2, -125z_2, 0)\text{.}\) Thus the generalized eigenvectors of \(T\) corresponding to the eigenvalue \(5\) are the nonzero vectors of the form \((0,0,z_3)\text{.}\)
🔗
The paragraphs above show that each of the standard basis vectors of \(\C3\) is a generalized eigenvector of \(T{}\text{.}\) Thus \(\C3\) indeed has a basis consisting of generalized eigenvectors of \(T{}\text{,}\) as promised by Theorem 8.9.
