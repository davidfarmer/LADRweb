Example 6.34. an orthonormal basis of \(\P_2(\R{})\).

Suppose we make \(\P{}_2(\R{})\) into an inner product space using the inner product given by
\begin{equation*} \langle p, q \rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} pq \end{equation*}
for all \(p, q \in \P{}_2(\R{})\text{.}\) We know that \(1, x, x^2\) is a basis of \(\P{}_2(\R{})\text{,}\) but it is not an orthonormal basis. We will find an orthonormal basis of \(\P{}_2(\R{})\) by applying the Gram–Schmidt procedure with \(v_1 = 1\text{,}\) \(v_2 = x\text{,}\) and \(v_3 = x^2{}\text{.}\)
🔗
To get started, take \(f_1 = v_1 = 1\text{.}\) Thus \(\lVert f_1 \rVert ^2 = \int_{-1}^1 1 = 2\text{.}\) Hence the formula in Theorem 6.32 tells us that
\begin{equation*} f_2 = v_2 - \frac{\langle v_2, f_1 \rangle }{\lVert f_1 \rVert ^2} f_1 = x - \frac{\langle x, 1 \rangle }{\lVert f_1 \rVert ^2} = x, \end{equation*}
where the last equality holds because \(\langle x, 1 \rangle = \int_{-1}^1 t dt = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
The formula above for \(f_2\) implies that \(\lVert f_2 \rVert ^2 = \int_{-1}^1 t^2 dt = \frac{2}{3}\text{.}\) Now the formula in Theorem 6.32 tells us that
\begin{align*} f_3 =\mathstrut\amp v_3 - \frac{\langle v_3, f_1 \rangle }{\lVert f_1 \rVert ^2} f_1 - \frac{\langle v_3, f_2 \rangle }{\lVert f_2 \rVert ^2} f_2 \\ =\mathstrut\amp x^2 - \tfrac{1}{2} \langle x^2{}, 1 \rangle - \tfrac{3}{2}\langle x^2{}, x \rangle x \\ =\mathstrut\amp x^2 - \tfrac{1}{3}\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗
The formula above for \(f_3\) implies that
\begin{equation*} \lVert f_3 \rVert ^2 = \int_{-1}^1 ( t^2 - \tfrac{1}{3} )^2 dt = \int_{-1}^{1} ( t^4 -\tfrac{2}{3} t^2 + \tfrac{1}{9} ) dt = \tfrac{8}{45}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Now dividing each of \(f_1, f_2, f_3\) by its norm gives us the orthonormal list
\begin{equation*} \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}}, \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}} x, \sqrt{\tfrac{45}{8}} ( x^2 - \tfrac{1}{3} ). \end{equation*}
🔗
The orthonormal list above has length three, which is the dimension of \(\P{}_2(\R{})\text{.}\) Hence this orthonormal list is an orthonormal basis of \(\P{}_2(\R{})\) [by Theorem 6.28].
