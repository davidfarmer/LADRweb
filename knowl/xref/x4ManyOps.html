Exercise 9.

Suppose \(V\) is a finite-dimensional nonzero complex vector space. Suppose that \(\E \subset \Lc(V)\) is such that \(S\) and \(T\) commute for all \(S, T \in \E\text{.}\)
  1. Prove that there is a vector in \(V\) that is an eigenvector for every element of \(\E\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Prove that there is a basis of \(V\) with respect to which every element of \(\E\) has an upper-triangular matrix.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Note: This exercise extends 5.78 and Theorem 5.80, which consider the case in which \(\E\) contains only two elements. For this exercise, \(\E\) may contain any number of elements, and \(\E\) may even be an infinite set.
