Exercise 12.

Suppose that \(V{}_1, \ldots, V{}_m\) are finite-dimensional inner product spaces. Prove that there is a unique inner product on \(V{}_1 \tensor \cdots \tensor V{}_m\) such that
\begin{equation*} \langle v_1 \tensor \cdots \tensor v_m, u_1 \tensor \cdots \tensor u_m \rangle = \langle v_1, u_1 \rangle \cdots \langle v_m, u_m \rangle \end{equation*}
for all \((v_1, \ldots, v_m)\) and \((u_1, \ldots, u_m)\) in \(V{}_1 \times \cdots \times V{}_m\text{.}\) Note: Note that the equation above implies that
\begin{equation*} \lVert v_1 \tensor \cdots \tensor v_m \rVert = \lVert v_1 \rVert \times \cdots \times \lVert v_m \rVert \end{equation*}
for all \((v_1, \ldots, v_m) \in V{}_1 \times \cdots \times V{}_m\text{.}\)
