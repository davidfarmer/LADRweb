Example 5.25. An operator whose eigenvalues cannot be found exactly.

Let \(T \in \Lc( \C{5} )\) be the operator defined by
\begin{equation*} T(z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4, z_5) = (-3z_5, z_1 + 6z_5, z_2, z_3, z_4). \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix of \(T\) with respect to the standard basis of \(\C5\) is the \(5\)-by-\(5\) matrix in Example 5.23. As we showed in that example, the minimal polynomial of \(T\) is the polynomial
\begin{equation*} 3 - 6z + z^5{}. \end{equation*}
🔗
No zero of the polynomial above can be expressed using rational numbers, roots of rational numbers, and the usual rules of arithmetic (a proof of this would take us considerably beyond linear algebra). Because the zeros of the polynomial above are the eigenvalues of \(T\) [by Theorem 5.24(a)] , we cannot find an exact expression for any eigenvalue of \(T\) in any familiar form.
🔗
Numeric techniques, which we will not discuss here, show that the zeros of the polynomial above, and thus the eigenvalues of \(T\text{,}\) are approximately the following five complex numbers:
\begin{equation*} -1.67, 0.51, 1.40, -0.12 + 1.59i, -0.12 - 1.59i. \end{equation*}
🔗
Note that the two nonreal zeros of this polynomial are complex conjugates of each other, as we expect for a polynomial with real coefficients (see 4.11).
