Example 3.89. sum of a vector and a one-dimensional subspace of \(\R2\).

Suppose
\begin{equation*} U = \{ (x, 2x) \in \R2: x \in \R{} \}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Hence \(U\) is the line in \(\R2\) through the origin with slope \(2\text{.}\) Thus
\begin{equation*} (17,20) + U \end{equation*}
is the line in \(\R2\) that contains the point \((17,20)\) and has slope \(2\text{.}\)
🔗
Because
\begin{equation*} (10, 20) \in U \andd (17,20) \in (17, 20) + U, \end{equation*}
we see that \((17,20) + U\) is obtained by moving \(U\) to the right by \(7\) units.
