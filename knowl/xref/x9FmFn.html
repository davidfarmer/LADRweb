Example 9.76. tensor product of element of \(\F m\) with element of \(\F n\).

Suppose \(m\) and \(n\) are positive integers. Let \(e_1, \ldots, e_m\) denote the standard basis of \(\F m\) and let \(f_1, \ldots, f_n\) denote the standard basis of \(\FF n\text{.}\) Suppose
\begin{equation*} v = (v_1, \ldots, v_m) \in \F m \andd w = (w_1, \ldots, w_n) \in \F n. \end{equation*}
🔗
Then
\begin{align*} v \tensor w \amp = ( \sum_{j=1}^m v_j e_j ) \tensor ( \sum_{k=1}^n w_k f_k )\\ \amp = \sum_{k=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m (v_j w_k) ( e_j \tensor f_k )\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗
Thus with respect to the basis \(\{ e_j \tensor f_k \}_{j = 1, \ldots, m; k = 1, \ldots, n}\) of \(\F m \tensor \F n\) provided by Theorem 9.74(b), the coefficients of \(v \tensor w\) are the numbers \(\{ v_j w_k \}_{j = 1, \ldots, m; k = 1, \ldots, n}\text{.}\) If instead of writing these numbers in a list, we write them in an \(m\)-by-\(n\) matrix with \(v_j w_k\) in row \(j\text{,}\) column \(k\text{,}\) then we can identify \(v \tensor w\) with the \(m\)-by-\(n\) matrix
\begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix}v_1 w_1 \amp \cdots \amp v_1 w_n \\ \amp \ddots \amp \\ v_m w_1 \amp \cdots \amp v_m w_n \end{pmatrix} . \end{equation*}
