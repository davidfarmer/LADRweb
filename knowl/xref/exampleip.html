Example 6.3. inner products.

  1. The Euclidean inner product on \(\F{n}\) is defined by
    \begin{equation*} \langle (w_1, \dots, w_n), (z_1, \dots, z_n) \rangle = w_1 \overline{z_1}+ \dots + w_n \overline{z_n} \end{equation*}
    for all \((w_1, \dots, w_n), (z_1, \dots, z_n) \in \FF n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If \(c_1, \dots, c_n\) are positive numbers, then an inner product can be defined on \(\F{n}\) by
    \begin{equation*} \langle (w_1, \dots, w_n), (z_1, \dots, z_n) \rangle = c_1 w_1 \overline{z_1} + \dots + c_n w_n \overline{z_n} \end{equation*}
    for all \((w_1, \dots, w_n), (z_1, \dots, z_n) \in \FF n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. An inner product can be defined on the vector space of continuous real-valued functions on the interval \([-1, 1]\) by
    \begin{equation*} \langle f, g \rangle = \int_{-1}^1fg \end{equation*}
    for all \(f, g\) continuous real-valued functions on \([-1,1]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. An inner product can be defined on \(\P(\R{})\) by
    \begin{equation*} \langle p, q \rangle = p(0) q(0) + \int_{-1}^1 p' q' \end{equation*}
    for all \(p, q \in \P(\R{})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. An inner product can be defined on \(\P(\R{})\) by
    \begin{equation*} \langle p, q \rangle = \int_0^\infty p(x) q(x) e^{-x} dx \end{equation*}
    for all \(p, q \in \P(\R{})\text{.}\)
