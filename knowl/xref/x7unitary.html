Exercise 27.

Suppose \(T \in \Lc(V)\) and \(s_1, \ldots, s_n\) are the singular values of \(T{}\text{.}\) Let \(e_1, \ldots, e_n\) and \(f_1, \ldots, f_n\) be orthonormal bases of \(V\) such that
\begin{equation*} Tv = s_1\langle v, e_1 \rangle f_1 + \cdots + s_n \langle v, e_n \rangle f_n \end{equation*}
for all \(v \in V{}\text{.}\) Define \(S \in \Lc(V)\) by
\begin{equation*} Sv = \langle v, e_1 \rangle f_1 + \cdots + \langle v, e_n \rangle f_n. \end{equation*}
  1. Show that \(S\) is unitary and \(\lVert T-S \rVert = \max \{ \lvert s_1 -1 \rvert , \ldots, \lvert s_n - 1 \rvert \}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that if \(E \in \Lc(V)\) is unitary, then \(\lVert T-E \rVert \ge \lVert T-S \rVert\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Note: This exercise finds a unitary operator \(S\) that is as close as possible (among the unitary operators) to a given operator \(T{}\text{.}\)
