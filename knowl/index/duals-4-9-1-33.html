Exercise 32.

The double dual space of \(V{}\text{,}\) denoted by \(V''\!\text{,}\) is defined to be the dual space of \(V'\!\text{.}\) In other words, \(V'' = {( V' )}'\text{.}\) Define \(\Lambda \colon V \to V''\) by
\begin{equation*} (\Lambda v)(\phi) = \phi(v) \end{equation*}
for each \(v \in V\) and each \(\phi \in V'\!\text{.}\)
  1. Show that \(\Lambda\) is a linear map from \(V\) to \(V''\!\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that if \(T \in \Lc(V)\text{,}\) then \(T'' \circ \Lambda = \Lambda \circ T\text{,}\) where \(T'' = {( T' )}'\!\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Show that if \(V\) is finite-dimensional, then \(\Lambda\) is an isomorphism from \(V\) onto \(V''\!\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Note: Suppose \(V\) is finite-dimensional. Then \(V\) and \(V'\) are isomorphic, but finding an isomorphism from \(V\) onto \(V'\) generally requires choosing a basis of \(V{}\text{.}\) In contrast, the isomorphism \(\Lambda\) from \(V\) onto \(V''\) does not require a choice of basis and thus is considered more natural.
