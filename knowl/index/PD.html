Example 5.72. partial differentiation operators commute.

Suppose \(m\) is a nonnegative integer. Let \(\P\!_m{}(\CC2, \C{})\) denote the complex vector space of polynomials (with coefficients in \(\C{}\)) in two variables and of degree at most \(m\text{,}\) with the usual operations of addition and scalar multiplication of \(\C{}\)-valued functions. Thus the elements of \(\P\!_m{}(\CC2, \C{})\) are functions \(p\) from \(\C2\) to \(\C{}\) of the form
\begin{equation} p(w,z) = \sum_{j+k \le m} a_{j,k} w^j z^k\!,\tag{5.73} \end{equation}
where the indices \(j\) and \(k\) take on all nonnegative integer values such that \(j + k \le m\text{,}\) each \(a_{j,k}\) is in \(\C{}\text{,}\) and \(w^j z^k\) denotes the function on \(\C2\) defined by \((w, z) \mapsto w^j z^k\!\text{.}\)
🔗
Define operators \(D_w, D_z \in \Lc( \P\!_m{}(\CC2, \C{}) )\) by
\begin{equation*} D_w p = \frac{ \partial p}{\partial w} = \sum_{j+k \le m} j a_{j,k} w^{j-1} z^k \andd D_z p = \frac{ \partial p}{\partial z} = \sum_{j+k \le m} k a_{j,k} w^j z^{k-1}\!, \end{equation*}
where \(p\) is as in (5.73). The operators \(D_w\) and \(D_z\) are called partial differentiation operators because each of these operators differentiates with respect to one of the variables while pretending that the other variable is a constant.
🔗
The operators \(D_w\) and \(D_z\) commute because if \(p\) is as in (5.73), then
\begin{equation*} (D_w D_z)p = \sum_{j+k \le m} j k a_{j,k} w^{j-1} z^{k-1} = (D_z D_w)p. \end{equation*}
🔗
The equation \(D_w D_z = D_z D_w\) on \(\P\!_m{}(\CC2, \C{})\) illustrates a more general result that the order of partial differentiation does not matter for nice functions.
