Exercise 19.

Suppose \(n\) is a positive integer. For this exercise, we adopt the notation that a typical element \(z\) of \(\C n\) is denoted by \(z = (z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_{n-1})\text{.}\) Define linear functionals \(\omega_0, \omega_1, \ldots, \omega_{n-1}\) on \(\C n\) by
\begin{equation*} \omega_j(z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_{n-1}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{m=0}^{n-1} z_m e^{-2\pi i j m/n}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The discrete Fourier transform is the operator \(\fun \CF {\C n} {\C n}\) defined by
\begin{equation*} \CF z = ( \omega_0(z), \omega_1(z), \ldots, \omega_{n-1}(z) ). \end{equation*}
  1. Show that \(\CF\) is a unitary operator on \(\CC n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that if \((z_0, \ldots, z_{n-1}) \in \C n\) and \(z_n\) is defined to equal \(z_0\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} \CF^{-1}(z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_{n-1}) = \CF(z_n, z_{n-1}, \ldots, z_1). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Show that \(\CF^4 = I\text{.}\)

    🔗

Note: The discrete Fourier transform has many important applications in data analysis. The usual Fourier transform involves expressions of the form \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x) e^{-2\pi i t x} dx\) for complex-valued integrable functions \(f\) defined on \(\R{}\text{.}\)
